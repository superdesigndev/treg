Why JSON, and why the model still matters

GPT Image 2.5 and Gemini 3 Pro on a plain prompt both looked wrong: eyes slightly too big, a doll-ish face, that over-sharpened HDR look that screams AI. Every image model has defaults it falls back to. A JSON prompt that pins face structure, skin texture, lighting, camera, expression, clothing and background leaves it no room to revert to them.

Same JSON across five models still gives five different answers. GPT Image 2.5 keeps the doll pattern in the eyes. Gemini 3 Pro gave the most realistic result. Pick the winner by looking, not by benchmark.

ugc-talking-head-video skill ↗

Seedance 2.5 less restriction mode

Feed the character image to Seedance 2.5 as an image reference, give it the script and a short audio clip for the rhythm. One image in, a talking head out: lip sync, blinks, hand gestures.

Seedance refuses hyper-realistic reference images to stop people impersonating real humans. Aggregators expose a bypass and charge double or triple for it. The proper way is BytePlus's advanced creation rights: upload the image, register it as a digital character, generate from it. Several hurdles to get approved. On treg that whole process is wrapped: the Seedance 2.5 less-restricted endpoint takes your image, registers it as a character asset, and generates. Same price as buying from BytePlus directly.

The voice trick

The default voice is off in rhythm, not fake exactly. Clip the audio from the trending video you liked and pass it as an audio reference. Too many details in speech to describe in text, so let the model copy them.

How the hooks are found

Give the agent a few reference accounts doing this well, ask it to pull their top videos and extract the hooks. Models are good at reading transcripts now. The pattern in this niche: a stunning claim, a specific number, and a result.

Ask it to brainstorm in that pattern, pick the lines you like, then one prompt per hook: Seedance 2.5, the character image, the audio reference. The ugc-talking-head-video skill does the whole pass: takes the request, writes the script and sets the duration, grabs the voice reference, writes the Seedance prompt, adds captions and headers, and outputs the final video.
